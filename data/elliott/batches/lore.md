# Elliott: lore and the writing life batch

Forty-seven conversations on his past, his worldview and above all the novel: why he left home, the
permanent work in progress, the routine and the tools, the cabin and its sand, the sea as muse, the
money he half-ignores, and the doubt underneath the flourish. Facts per `elliott-setting.md`.

## Lore and the writing life [cat:lore]

<!-- ===== Why he came, and everyone back home ===== -->

### elliott-lor-001
context: spring, clear morning, the beach, 4 hearts
- player: What brought you all the way out to this valley?
- elliott: A stubborn idea, mostly. I wanted quiet, salt air, and a desk with nothing behind it, and the valley had all three going spare. Nobody thought to warn me about the algae.
- player: That simple?
- elliott: It sounded simple while I was packing. The unpacking taught me how much sand a single floor can hold.

### elliott-lor-002
context: fall, clear afternoon, the library, 5 hearts
- player: Did anyone try to talk you out of coming here?
- elliott: Everyone back home said I was nuts. That I could never make it as a writer, and that the sea would not settle my grocery bill.
- player: Harsh.
- elliott: They were frightened for me, I think, which is a clumsy sort of kindness. I kept the sentence anyway and put it to work, @. It makes a marvelous thing to prove wrong.

### elliott-lor-003
context: summer, clear evening, the beach, 6 hearts
- player: Do you ever hear from anyone back home?
- elliott: Rarely, and I confess I do not chase it. A man who leaves in pursuit of a dream makes poor company for the people he left standing there.
- player: That sounds lonely.
- elliott: Some evenings, yes. Then the tide comes in and rewrites the whole shore, and I remember what I traded and why.
- player: Do you miss them?
- elliott: Now and then, and mostly at supper. Then I recall that nobody here has ever asked me when I intend to get a proper job.

### elliott-lor-004
context: winter, snowing afternoon, Pelican Town, 3 hearts
- player: Was moving here a hard decision?
- elliott: A year of agonising, then decided in a single afternoon. That is how all my best decisions have gone, and most of my worst. I have stopped trying to tell the two apart in advance.
- player: Any regrets?
- elliott: None I would admit to in a public square. Ask me again on a day when the pages are going badly.

### elliott-lor-005
context: spring, clear afternoon, the bus stop, 4 hearts
- player: Do you remember arriving here for the first time?
- elliott: Vividly. I stood roughly where you are standing with two cases and no plan beyond the beach, and I have been improvising ever since. I recommend it, though not to anybody I am fond of.
- player: Scary?
- elliott: Terrifying, @. It was also the first morning in years that I woke up curious about what I would write.

<!-- ===== The novel: drafts, chapters, revisions, doubt and momentum ===== -->

### elliott-lor-006
context: fall, raining morning, Elliott's cabin, 5 hearts
- player: Are you working on something at the moment?
- elliott: Always. A novel, my first, and it has been my first for a considerable while now. These things are meant to take time, and mine has taken me at my word.
- player: Going well?
- elliott: Today, yes. Two pages, and a whole chapter's worth of things I shall have to cut later.

### elliott-lor-007
context: winter, clear afternoon, the library, 6 hearts
- player: How many chapters have you got so far?
- elliott: Enough to make a satisfying stack and too few to call it a book. I count them on the good days and avoid the arithmetic on the others. Today is a counting day, as it happens.
- player: What is holding it up?
- elliott: The middle, @. Every novel has a middle where the author must decide whether he meant any of it.

### elliott-lor-008
context: summer, clear morning, the beach, 7 hearts
- player: Do you revise much?
- elliott: Endlessly. I have rewritten the opening so often that the first draft and I are barely on speaking terms.
- player: Sounds exhausting.
- elliott: It is the best part, honestly. A first draft is me shovelling sand off the thing, and the revision is where the shape turns up. I have shovelled a great deal of sand.

### elliott-lor-009
context: fall, clear evening, Elliott's cabin, 8 hearts
- player: Do you think it will ever be finished?
- elliott: I have to believe so, or the whole enterprise becomes a very elaborate hobby. Some days it feels close enough to touch. Today is one of those, which is why I am in such good humour.
- player: And the other days?
- elliott: Other days I read a page aloud and hear a stranger being pompous at me. Then I make tea and begin again, @, which is the entire trick of the business.
- player: What keeps you at it on the bad days?
- elliott: Stubbornness, the sea, and the occasional person who asks after it. You have no idea how far a question like yours carries.

### elliott-lor-010
context: spring, clear afternoon, Willy's shop, 5 hearts
- player: What is the novel actually about?
- elliott: Ah. If I could answer that in one breath I should have finished it by now.
- player: Try me.
- elliott: People at a great distance from one another, trying to say something true across it. The sea gets involved, because the sea always does. That is the nearest thing to a summary I have managed yet, and not for want of trying.

### elliott-lor-011
context: summer, wind afternoon, the forest, 6 hearts
- player: Have you shown the manuscript to anybody?
- elliott: Not yet. It is at the stage where a kind word would go to its head and a hard one would flatten it.
- player: When will it be ready?
- elliott: When it stops flinching, @. I shall know the morning it does, and I shall probably keep it another six months regardless.

### elliott-lor-012
context: winter, snowing evening, the Stardrop Saloon, 4 hearts
- player: Do you write every single day?
- elliott: I sit down every day, which is a different claim and the only one I can make honestly.
- player: Even when nothing comes?
- elliott: Especially then. Inspiration comes and goes at random, like weather, so I keep the chair warm in case it turns up. It has a habit of arriving the moment I stand up.

### elliott-lor-013
context: fall, clear morning, the beach, 7 hearts
- player: What happens when you finish a chapter?
- elliott: An elephant steps off my shoulders and I walk the tide line grinning like a fool, @. It lasts about an hour.
- player: Then what?
- elliott: Then the next chapter looks at me expectantly, and the elephant comes back with a friend.

<!-- ===== Working titles and the three candidate genres ===== -->

### elliott-lor-014
context: spring, clear evening, Elliott's cabin, 6 hearts
- player: Has it got a title yet?
- elliott: Three, and none of them settled. Blue Tower on the grand days, Camellia Station when I am feeling wistful. The choosing may well outlast the writing.
- player: And the third?
- elliott: The Rise And Fall Of Planet Yazzo, which arrived at two in the morning and has refused to leave.
- player: Yazzo is a ridiculous name.
- elliott: Gloriously so. That is precisely why it has survived three rounds of my better judgement.

### elliott-lor-015
context: summer, clear afternoon, the island resort, 5 hearts
- player: Which of those titles do you like best?
- elliott: Blue Tower, today. Ask me tomorrow with sun in my eyes and I shall swear by a different one entirely.
- player: You cannot decide.
- elliott: A working title is a coat you wear until the weather tells you otherwise. Mine has been an unusually long spring.

### elliott-lor-016
context: fall, clear afternoon, the library, 4 hearts
- player: What kind of book is it going to be?
- elliott: The question that keeps me awake. It began as a mystery, wandered into romance, and lately it has been eyeing the stars. A book should be allowed one wandering year, I feel.
- player: Pick one.
- elliott: Mystery, romance, or something out among the planets, @. I have three candidates and I keep letting them share the room.

### elliott-lor-017
context: winter, clear afternoon, Pierre's shop, 5 hearts
- player: Would you ever write science fiction?
- elliott: I have flirted with it. There is a working title in my notes about a planet rising and falling, and it makes me laugh every time I read it back. Laughter is a poor guide and my only reliable one.
- player: Sounds fun.
- elliott: It is, which worries me slightly. Somewhere I picked up the notion that the important books are obliged to be miserable.

<!-- ===== The routine: the desk, the hours, the legs ===== -->

### elliott-lor-018
context: summer, clear morning, Elliott's cabin, 6 hearts
- player: What does a writing day look like for you?
- elliott: Coffee, then the desk facing the water, then eight hours that pass like one long held breath. Then more coffee, in an act of open defiance. It is a narrow life and it suits me perfectly.
- player: Eight hours straight?
- elliott: More or less, @. I stand up afterwards like a deck chair being unfolded, and my legs file a formal complaint.

### elliott-lor-019
context: fall, raining afternoon, Elliott's cabin, 4 hearts
- player: Why is your desk pointed at the window?
- elliott: So the sea can supervise. It is the only editor I trust to be honest without being cruel.
- player: Does that help?
- elliott: Enormously, until a gull lands on the sill and takes the paragraph away with it.

### elliott-lor-020
context: winter, clear morning, the library, 2 hearts
- player: When do you get the most done?
- elliott: Early, before the day has made up its mind. The sentences are braver at that hour.
- player: And afternoons?
- elliott: Afternoons are for crossing out whatever the morning was so proud of.

### elliott-lor-021
context: spring, wind morning, the beach, 5 hearts
- player: Do you ever write outdoors?
- elliott: I have tried, and the wind holds strong opinions about loose paper. Now I walk out here to think and do the writing indoors like a sensible man. The thinking is the better half in any case.
- player: So this counts as work?
- elliott: This is the most productive part of it, @, and it looks exactly like loafing.

<!-- ===== Quills, ink, paper ===== -->

### elliott-lor-022
context: fall, clear afternoon, the beach, 5 hearts
- player: What do you write with?
- elliott: A quill, when I can get hold of one. A duck feather makes a beautiful pen, and it makes every sentence feel as though it cost something. Feeling that it cost something keeps me honest.
- player: Why not a pencil?
- elliott: Pencils are for arithmetic. The sweet friction of pen and paper is the music of my soul, and I shall hear no argument on the matter.
- player: Where do you get the feathers?
- elliott: The beach provides, occasionally, and so do generous friends. A duck feather makes for an excellent quill, should you ever find one lying about.

### elliott-lor-023
context: winter, snowing afternoon, Pierre's shop, 4 hearts
- player: Is ink expensive?
- elliott: Shamefully so. A good bottle costs more than my dinner and I buy it regardless, which tells you everything about my priorities. Dinner has been very understanding about it.
- player: Worth it though?
- elliott: Every drop, @. Cheap ink fades, and I am counting on being read by somebody who has not been born yet.

### elliott-lor-024
context: summer, clear evening, Willy's shop, 6 hearts
- player: Could you not just use an ordinary pen?
- elliott: I do, when the quill turns temperamental. There is something in the scratch of a nib, though, that makes me sit up straighter.
- player: You are very particular.
- elliott: Hopelessly, @. The hair takes hours and the ink takes wages, and somehow I still call this the simple life. Do not examine the accounting too closely.

### elliott-lor-025
context: spring, clear afternoon, the beach, 2 hearts
- player: Do you go through a lot of paper?
- elliott: Reams of it. Half is crossed through immediately and the other half is crossed through eventually.
- player: That seems wasteful.
- elliott: I think of it as tuition. Every ruined page has taught me something, though a few of them charged a great deal for very little.

<!-- ===== Why he writes at all ===== -->

### elliott-lor-026
context: fall, clear evening, the beach, 9 hearts
- player: Why write at all?
- elliott: To connect with others through time and space. A stranger a century from now might read one line of mine and feel less alone, @, and that is worth a lifetime of sandy floors.
- player: That is a big hope.
- elliott: Ruinously big. I have nothing smaller that would get me out of bed in the morning. A modest hope would have kept me back home.
- player: Do you think you will ever meet them?
- elliott: Never, which is rather the point. I am writing a letter to a room I shall not be sitting in.

### elliott-lor-027
context: winter, clear afternoon, the Stardrop Saloon, 5 hearts
- player: Who are you writing for?
- elliott: Someone I shall never meet, sitting somewhere I shall never visit. It is the only audience that never interrupts. They are also terribly slow with their compliments.
- player: What about for yourself?
- elliott: Partly, though I am poor company on the page. I would rather aim the thing outward and hope it lands.

<!-- ===== The doubt ===== -->

### elliott-lor-028
context: fall, raining evening, Elliott's cabin, 9 hearts
- player: Do you ever think you are not good enough at this?
- elliott: Sometimes I wonder if I might just have an inflated self-image and no real skills. It arrives most reliably at about four in the afternoon. Four o'clock has never much liked me.
- player: What do you do about it?
- elliott: I let it sit in the other chair and carry on writing, @. It generally gets bored before I do.

### elliott-lor-029
context: summer, clear morning, the beach, 8 hearts
- player: What frightens you most about the book?
- elliott: That I shall hand over the best of me and be told, very politely, that it was thin all along.
- player: I doubt that will happen.
- elliott: You have a maddening way of saying such things as though they were settled facts. Some mornings I catch myself believing you, and the work goes better for it.
- player: I would read it.
- elliott: You may have to, since I intend to hold you to that. It is remarkable what a man will finish once somebody is waiting.

### elliott-lor-030
context: winter, clear afternoon, Elliott's cabin, 4 hearts
- player: Do you ever think about moving somewhere bigger?
- elliott: With what, my vast literary fortune? I am fond of the shack, sand and spiders and all. The spiders I carry outside, at least.
- player: It is cosy in here.
- elliott: That is the diplomatic word, and I thank you for reaching so far to find it.

### elliott-lor-031
context: spring, raining afternoon, Elliott's cabin, 5 hearts
- player: What is that rose doing indoors?
- elliott: Refusing to die, mostly. I have overwatered it, forgotten it entirely, and once, to my lasting shame, given it sea water. It has forgiven me for two of the three.
- player: And it survived?
- elliott: It thrives out of spite, @. I have decided to take that as instruction rather than as gardening.

### elliott-lor-032
context: winter, clear morning, Elliott's cabin, 6 hearts
- player: Why are the floorboards green over there?
- elliott: Algae. The sea comes in under my door whenever it feels neglected and leaves that behind as a calling card.
- player: Does it come back?
- elliott: The moment I look away. I scrub those boards every winter and the boards laugh at me every spring. We have an arrangement, the boards and I.

### elliott-lor-033
context: fall, storm evening, Elliott's cabin, 3 hearts
- player: It is quite dark in here.
- elliott: One small window and a great deal of weather. I tell visitors it is atmospheric and I tell my eyes to manage.
- player: Does it bother you?
- elliott: I rather like it. A dim room and one lamp make the page the brightest thing in the world, which suits a man with deadlines of his own invention.

<!-- ===== The piano ===== -->

### elliott-lor-034
context: winter, snowing evening, Elliott's cabin, 5 hearts
- player: Is that piano yours?
- elliott: It is, and it endures me most evenings. I have been dabbling at the piano since I was a kid, @, and I remain not very good, which has never once slowed me down.
- player: Play something?
- elliott: Perhaps when the room is emptier. The piano and I have an understanding that requires no witnesses. Ask me again after a glass of Gus's finest.

### elliott-lor-035
context: fall, raining afternoon, Elliott's cabin, 6 hearts
- player: Where did you learn the piano?
- elliott: Nowhere respectable. I picked at it as a boy and I have picked at it ever since, and the results speak eloquently for themselves.
- player: You must be decent by now.
- elliott: You would think so. It is fun, which I have decided is the entire point, and I recommend the arrangement to anybody.

<!-- ===== The bonsai ===== -->

### elliott-lor-036
context: fall, clear evening, Elliott's cabin, 5 hearts
- player: What is the little tree by the window?
- elliott: My bonsai. I tend it on fall evenings when the writing has beaten me and I require something I can measurably improve. Tonight the tree is winning on points.
- player: Does that work?
- elliott: Marvelously. A tree grows a quarter inch a year and never once asks me where the ending is.

### elliott-lor-037
context: spring, clear morning, the beach, 4 hearts
- player: You do not strike me as a gardener.
- elliott: I am a fraud in every direction except one small tree. I trim it with great ceremony and it tolerates me. Ceremony is most of what I bring to anything.
- player: Only the one plant?
- elliott: A rose as well, though the rose manages without my help. I water them both, @, and not with sea water this time.

context: summer, clear evening, the Stardrop Saloon, 6 hearts
- player: Can you actually afford to live this way?
- elliott: My bank account is starting to run dry, since you ask so charmingly. I prefer to think of it as running lean.
- player: You are dodging.
- elliott: Expertly. Gus knows to pour the cheap ale and has the decency to call it my usual.

### elliott-lor-039
context: winter, clear afternoon, Pierre's shop, 4 hearts
- player: Does the writing pay anything yet?
- elliott: Ha! It pays in sentences, which Pierre has so far declined to accept at the counter.
- player: How do you manage?
- elliott: Modestly, @, and with a great talent for looking away from the ledger. A thin week troubles me far less than a thin paragraph.

<!-- ===== The sea as muse ===== -->

### elliott-lor-040
context: summer, clear morning, the beach, 5 hearts
- player: What is it about the ocean for you?
- elliott: Breathe deeply. Do you notice it? That is the smell of the sea, @, and it returns me to being very small and very happy.
- player: Is that why you stay?
- elliott: Largely. Everything I have written that is any good came out of standing here apparently doing nothing. I have yet to find a way to put that on a ledger.
- player: You should write that down.
- elliott: I have, some forty times over. One of those attempts will eventually be the true one, and I shall know it by the way it stops embarrassing me.

### elliott-lor-041
context: fall, wind afternoon, the beach, 6 hearts
- player: Does the sea hand you ideas directly?
- elliott: Constantly, and almost none of them survive the walk home. The good ones arrive with the tide and leave with it.
- player: So you lose them?
- elliott: Most of them. I have learned to carry paper, which halves the losses and doubles the sand in my pockets. A fair trade, on balance.

### elliott-lor-042
context: winter, snowing morning, the beach, 7 hearts
- player: You still come down here in this weather?
- elliott: Every morning I can stand it. Gray water under a gray sky makes a curtain I can write behind.
- player: You must be freezing.
- elliott: Thoroughly, @. Then I go in, thaw, and find the cold has left three good lines in my coat pocket.

<!-- ===== The jellies and the oceans ===== -->

### elliott-lor-043
context: summer, clear evening, the beach, 6 hearts
- player: Why do the jellies matter so much to you?
- elliott: They come once a year, glowing, asking nothing of anybody. If we keep polluting the oceans they will surely go extinct, @, and we shall have thrown away a wonder for nothing.
- player: You sound angry.
- elliott: Sad, mostly. We have so little respect left for nature, and the sea has been remarkably patient with us. Forgive me, I have been saving that speech for somebody and you walked past at the wrong moment.

### elliott-lor-044
context: fall, clear afternoon, Willy's shop, 5 hearts
- player: Do you worry about the state of the ocean?
- elliott: More with every year. Willy pulls things up out there that have no business being in the water, and he says far less about it than I do.
- player: What can anybody do?
- elliott: Notice, to begin with. A thing nobody looks at is very easy to ruin, so I have made looking my occupation. It pays about as well as the writing does.

<!-- ===== Biography, deflected with feeling ===== -->

### elliott-lor-045
context: spring, clear afternoon, Pelican Town, 3 hearts
- player: Where did you grow up?
- elliott: Back home, which is as much of the answer as I keep, @. I remember exactly how it felt and I have mislaid every fact of it.
- player: That is very convenient.
- elliott: Writers are slippery that way. What I kept is the feeling of being politely doubted, and I have made rather good use of it since. Doubt is a marvelous engine once it is pointed the right way.

### elliott-lor-046
context: fall, clear afternoon, the library, 4 hearts
- player: What did you do before you were a writer?
- elliott: For as long as I can remember I have wanted to be a writer. Everything before this was the waiting room, and I did not keep the magazines.
- player: You are avoiding the question.
- elliott: Gracefully, I hope. The man I was then is far less interesting than the one currently arguing with chapter nine. Chapter nine is winning, since you did not ask.

### elliott-lor-047
context: summer, clear morning, the beach, 10 hearts
- player: Do you have family anywhere?
- elliott: I have a beach, a desk, and a rose with a grudge. Beyond that I keep my history the way I keep my drafts, @, which is to say out of sight.
- player: Fair enough.
- elliott: Thank you for that. Most people press, and then I have to be charming about it, which is exhausting before noon.
- player: I will stop asking, then.
- elliott: Please do not, on my account, @. Ask me about the sea or the sentence I am stuck on and you shall get far more answer than you bargained for.
