# Elliott: lore and the writing life batch

Forty-seven conversations on his past, his worldview and above all the novel: why he left home, the
permanent work in progress, the routine and the tools, the cabin and its sand, the sea as muse, the
money he half-ignores, and the doubt underneath the flourish. Facts per `elliott-setting.md`.

## Lore and the writing life [cat:lore]

<!-- ===== Why he came, and everyone back home ===== -->

### elliott-lor-001
context: spring, clear morning, the beach, 4 hearts
- player: What brought you all the way out to this valley?
- elliott: Quiet, mostly: I wanted salt air and a desk where I could finally write my novel. Nobody thought to warn me about the algae.
- player: That simple?
- elliott: Simple to say and harder to do. The unpacking alone taught me how much sand a single floor can hold.

### elliott-lor-002
context: fall, clear afternoon, the library, 5 hearts
- player: Did anyone try to talk you out of coming here?
- elliott: Yes, everyone back home. They said I was nuts and that I could never make it as a writer.
- player: Harsh.
- elliott: It was, though I think they were frightened for me, which is a clumsy sort of kindness. Their doubt makes a marvelous thing to prove wrong, @.

### elliott-lor-003
context: summer, clear evening, the beach, 6 hearts
- player: Do you ever hear from anyone back home?
- elliott: Rarely, and I confess I do not chase it. A man who leaves to chase a dream makes poor company for the people he left standing there.
- player: That sounds lonely.
- elliott: Some evenings it is, yes. Then the tide comes in and rewrites the whole shore, and I remember why I came.
- player: Do you miss them?
- elliott: Now and then, mostly at supper. Then I recall that nobody here has ever asked me when I intend to get a proper job.

### elliott-lor-004
context: winter, snowing afternoon, Pelican Town, 3 hearts
- player: Was moving here a hard decision?
- elliott: Yes: a year of agonizing, and then I decided in a single afternoon. Most of my best decisions have gone that way, and most of my worst.
- player: Any regrets?
- elliott: None, most days. Ask me again on a day when the pages are going badly.

### elliott-lor-005
context: spring, clear afternoon, the bus stop, 4 hearts

- elliott: Vividly. I stood about where you are standing with two cases and no plan beyond the beach.
- player: Scary?
- elliott: Terrifying, @. It was also the first morning in years that I woke up curious about what I would write.

### elliott-lor-009
context: fall, clear evening, Elliott's cabin, 8 hearts

- player: What keeps you at it on the bad days?

- elliott: Yes, it is the most productive part of it, @, and it looks exactly like loafing.

<!-- ===== Quills, ink, paper ===== -->

### elliott-lor-022
context: fall, clear afternoon, the beach, 5 hearts
- player: What do you write with?
- elliott: A quill when I can get hold of a duck feather, and bottled ink. It makes every sentence feel as though it cost something.
- player: Why not a pencil?
- elliott: I prefer ink; pencils are for arithmetic. The sweet friction of pen and paper is the music of my soul.
- player: Where do you get the feathers?
- elliott: From the beach now and then, and from generous friends. A duck feather makes for an excellent quill, should you ever find one lying about.

### elliott-lor-023
context: winter, snowing afternoon, Pierre's shop, 4 hearts
- player: Is ink expensive?
- elliott: Yes, shamefully so; a good bottle costs more than my dinner. I buy it regardless, which tells you everything about my priorities.
- player: Worth it though?
- elliott: Every drop, @. Cheap ink fades, and I am counting on being read by somebody who has not been born yet.

### elliott-lor-024
context: summer, clear evening, Willy's shop, 6 hearts
- player: Could you not just use an ordinary pen?
- elliott: I do, when the quill turns temperamental. There is something in the scratch of a nib, though, that makes me sit up straighter.
- player: You are very particular.
- elliott: Hopelessly, @. The hair takes hours and the ink takes wages, and somehow I still call this the simple life.

### elliott-lor-025

- player: That seems wasteful.
- elliott: It is, a little, but I think of it as tuition. Every ruined page has taught me something.

<!-- ===== Why he writes at all ===== -->

### elliott-lor-026
context: fall, clear evening, the beach, 9 hearts
- player: Why write at all?
- elliott: To connect with others through time and space. A stranger a century from now might read one line of mine and feel less alone, @, and that is worth a lifetime of sandy floors.
- player: That is a big hope.
- elliott: It is enormous, and nothing smaller would get me out of bed in the morning. A modest hope would have kept me back home.
- player: Do you think you will ever meet them?
- elliott: No, never, and I have made my peace with that. I am writing a letter to a room I shall not be sitting in.

### elliott-lor-027
context: winter, clear afternoon, the Stardrop Saloon, 5 hearts
- player: Who are you writing for?
- elliott: For a reader I shall never meet, sitting somewhere I shall never visit. They are terribly slow with their compliments.
- player: What about for yourself?
- elliott: Partly, yes, though I would rather aim the thing outward and hope it lands.

### elliott-lor-040
context: summer, clear morning, the beach, 5 hearts
- player: What is it about the ocean for you?

- elliott: Largely, yes. Everything I have written that is any good came out of standing here apparently doing nothing.

- player: You sound angry.
